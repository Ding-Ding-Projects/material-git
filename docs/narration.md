# Event narration and message style

Narration is off by default. Enable it in Voice and sound, then choose English, Hong Kong Cantonese or Both. Both speaks English followed by Cantonese strictly one utterance at a time. Each language has its own installed-voice picker; Choose automatically is the default. The application persists a voice URI, not its display name. Late enumeration updates the picker. A removed voice keeps its saved identity while falling back, a network-backed voice is identified, and a language without a matching installed voice is reported. Mandarin is never used as a Cantonese substitute. Rate is bounded to 0.5–2 and pitch to 0–2, with 1 as normal.

The narrator debounces routine events for 180 ms, replaces queued events in the same category and applies a four-second category cooldown after completion. Error events bypass those timing limits, and `announceMessage` preserves exact event facts while styling the surrounding language. Speech callbacks serialize the two language tracks. Cancellation invalidates old callbacks and clears pending speech. A screen reader or Quiet sound preference pauses app speech immediately. Browsers cannot reliably detect active screen readers, so the preference and this limitation are explicit. The application does not claim automatic assistive-technology detection.

English and Cantonese playfulness are independent, persisted levels from 1 (serious) to 5 (most playful), each shipped at 5. They style status, error, warning, destructive, financial, security, accessibility and progress messages. Facts remain exact at every level. Dialog emoji decoration is separate, non-semantic and absent from control labels and accessible names. Bilingual labels expose compact secondary text through `languageParts`; `t` offers a combined string for surfaces with room.

Application text is held in `localization-resources.ts`, separate from logic. `t` accepts resource identifiers; `localizeText` translates exact registered application-owned source strings only. Commands, provider records, voice names, URLs, paths, identifiers and external factual payloads remain verbatim. Personal-vocabulary replacements apply only to registered application-owned text, stay local, and are omitted from exports and history. A missing resource falls back to its original identifier rather than inventing a translation.

`tests/preferences.test.ts` verifies independent language levels, fact preservation in all message categories, voice identity/fallback, bilingual serialization, superseded events, cooldowns and screen-reader cancellation. Real system voice availability depends on the installed operating-system voices; mocked synthesis tests do not prove audible playback on a Windows device.
